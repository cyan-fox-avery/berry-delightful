## Current canon at a glance (2026-10-10; takes precedence over older proposals)
- **Names:** **Berry Delightful** is the game; **Starvale Farm** is the fictional farm, inspired by Stardale Farm.
- **Format:** lightweight browser-first visual-novel/point-and-click game; about 7–8 handcrafted playable scenes. No free-roaming avatar or heavyweight engine required.
- **Premise:** the player **buys** the childhood strawberry-picking farm after the last owner's adult child puts it up for sale. Once a beloved pick-your-own farm and market stand, it is restored by choice, not inherited as a duty.
- **Daily pacing:** **start without a per-day action limit** (Avery, 2026-10-10). Crop growth, stock and player choice pace the loop. Revisit a budget only if human testing reveals checklist-like repetitive days. The older four-action rule was a prototype trial, not the current default. Festival strolling likewise has no action slots.
- **Storms:** first major storm scripted early as a cooking/experimenting on-ramp; fields close during major storms, but crops are safe and the kitchen remains available. No replacement four-action quota. Additional bespoke storm moments are an open scope question.
- **Arc:** player-paced September 28 festival (gameplay payoff) → autumn giving/free play → Christmas letter and fictional in-game cultivar 'Fiona' (personal emotional finale). Year Two is an optional continuation **after** Christmas, not a condition for reaching the ending.
- **Personal touches:** Buddy is the real pig's name, offered as the naming default; Barbara's original berry patch remains at Barbara's house, with a runner given after the Barbara Special; the 27 jars are a visual-only easter egg; the kittens find homes and their mother is spayed.
- **Prototypes:** the grey box tests mechanics, not art. Ben's playtest and Mira's review gate graduation; any separate "Grey Box 2" build needs its own verified review. Other pitches are on pause at Avery's request.

## The game in one breath
A cozy, finite strawberry farming game for Avery's sister Fiona — "a small playable love letter to Fiona: strawberries, sunshine, baking, growing something carefully, making a place beautiful, and eventually sharing what you made with other people."

The player buys the neglected farm where they picked strawberries as a child and restores it into a beautiful working strawberry farm. Core loop: plant → tend → harvest → sell or process → improve the farm → unlock new varieties, growing methods, recipes, and possibilities.

## Structure
- **Act I (tutorial–tier 2): restoration.** The farm goes scruffy → neat → alive; the player learns the loop, builds volume, learns to bake. A private project.
- **Act II (tier 3+): the farm faces outward.** The roadside stand opens direct sales and starts the festival pipeline — the farm becomes part of the community, building toward something bigger.
- **Act III: festival preparation.** A qualitatively different final phase — not tier 8+. A finite checklist: choose/develop the signature creation, gather what it needs, practice it, prepare the farm for visitors, decorate, stock the stand. The main farming arc converges on the festival (the gameplay win), followed by autumn free play and the separate Christmas emotional finale. (Adopted from ChatGPT's review, 2026-10-06. Design note from Milo, 2026-10-06: the phase should feel like the farm *showing off* everything the player built.)

## Season & time (DECIDED 2026-10-06; festival timing refined 2026-10-07)
- Longer season: late June → late September (~90 days), player-paced — early summer to early fall.
- **Festival inspiration: the Vankleek Hill Fair** (Avery, 2026-10-07) — the agricultural fair in Vankleek Hill, Prescott-Russell, Ontario, running 182 years ("Small Town – Big Hearts"). The nostalgia reference for the in-game festival's feel: a multi-day small-town fair — opening parade, artisan/homecraft exhibit hall (baking, preserves), petting zoo, midway, demolition derby, antique tractor pulls, the whole community turning out. The game festival is a strawberry festival, but its heartbeat is this: a small-town ag fair at the most beautiful week of the year. The world itself turns toward autumn as Act III approaches: leaves going red and gold during the countdown, the valley at its most beautiful on festival day. The day-neutral strawberries (Seascape, Albion) are still producing right up to the festival — the season's real-world logic holds.
- The Strawberry Festival is NOT on a fixed date *mechanically*. It is a **countdown triggered when a specific set of conditions is met** — the trigger is the Act II → Act III transition, and the countdown window IS Act III (the festival-prep checklist). The season is a container, not a deadline; the finale arrives on the player's terms. No fail state. **Narratively, the festival is always September 28** — Fiona's birthday (Mira's Option B, adopted 2026-10-07): the game's day count is elastic, not a literal date-by-date calendar. September 28 stays meaningful without ever becoming a deadline.
- **The Festival Committee's Readiness List** (DECIDED 2026-10-06; shown in-game from Act II): 1) Starvale fully restored (complete the T7 tree); 2) roadside stand open and serving; 3) a signature festival creation baked (the **Gariguette Fraisier** — named 2026-10-06; the "Berry Famous" moment). All three met → formal invitation → **14-day countdown** → festival day as a short playable vignette (walk the fair, present the creation, spot the cameos) → free play continues after. Structural milestones only, nothing grindy. (Option A DECIDED 2026-10-06: T7 completes Act II — the festival waits for the full tree rather than triggering at T6.)
- **Free play is a giving phase, not an earning phase** (Milo's pitch, adopted 2026-10-07 — Avery: "it's excellent"): the festival day is designed, but the epilogue needed a verb — so the verb flips from *earning* to *giving*. Three beats, all reusing existing UI: 1) **Request board at the roadside stand** — small gift deliveries for family and community (a jar of Bounty jam for a neighbour's newborn, berries for a church bake sale). No currency, no reputation meter, no checklist — the reward is a thank-you note that lands in the cookbook's scrapbook section. Giving, not questing. **Requests are small and from unnamed customers** (Avery, 2026-10-10 — counter to Milo's named-regulars pitch): the writer is readable through handwriting, never named — really nice handwriting, probably an adult; messy scrawl with a couple of minor spelling errors, a kid; an all-caps scrawl (not yelling, just not wasting time on pretty), a fellow farmer wanting to try the berries. The deduction is the pleasure; no relationship tracking, no per-regular arcs. 2) **The stand goes free** — after the festival, selling stops: "take some, leave a smile." The stand's stock slowly drains; the pig gets the surplus — pig's verdict, for the official record: **"Surplus Disposal: Excellent."** The economy visibly sunsets itself — the numbers stop mattering because the game is over. 3) **The memory room completes the circle** — missed keepsakes arrive gently in the epilogue as gifts (Barbara presses the festival program into your hands; thank-you notes pin themselves up). Not a checklist; a closing. Guardrail (Mira, 2026-10-07): the epilogue is generous but honest — it delivers missed mementoes, never trophies for things the player didn't do. That protects the meaning of both discovery and generosity. This also settles the keepsake-discoverability question: no telegraphing during the season is needed, because the epilogue finishes the collection — the "no checklist" rule stays pure and nothing is permanently missable. The season's thematic realization is what the game's been saying all along: the berries were never the point. The personal Christmas finale follows later, without requiring the player to complete a postgame checklist.
- **The Christmas finale (Avery, 2026-10-09 — the game's true ending, after free play):** winter comes; the game ends at Christmas on a silent, snowy farm. The fourth wall breaks a little: Fiona gets a letter from her brother — Merry Christmas, and she is very loved. The pig wears a little scarf. The game closes with the present from the unnamed brother: a new breed of strawberry, named **'Fiona'** — *Fragaria × ananassa* 'Fiona' (DECIDED 2026-10-09 — Avery). 'Fiona' is a fictional in-game cultivar name; a preliminary search is not proof of real-world name availability. The Latin name is a field-guide beat: real cultivars hang off the species like surnames, and this one hangs off hers. The game is Fiona's Christmas present, and the ending makes that literal — the season teaches "the berries were never the point," and the finale says the point outright, once. (Structural note: festival Sept 28 → free-play autumn → Christmas winter. Journal shelving happens **only if the player opts to begin another season after Christmas**, not as a prerequisite to the finale; the exact ritual is still under discussion.)
- **Years after the first (Avery, 2026-10-09):** the game can continue into a new year after Christmas — the farm resets for a new season, but scripted events change somewhat (never a rote repeat). And yes: the Fiona berries — the Christmas present cultivar — are plantable starting the next spring.
- **Nudge** (DECIDED 2026-10-06): if ~day 50 arrives with the list incomplete, the committee's letter arrives warmly noting what's missing — diegetic, no fail state.
- Day loop (proposed): one free action phase per day (tend, harvest, bake, sell, buy upgrades), closed by End day; plants grow overnight. New plantings take ~3–4 days to first fruit; June-bearers produce heavily in ~8-day windows; everbearers trickle all season; greenhouse enables out-of-season growing. One simple weather forecast per day (sun/rain/heat) — cultivar personalities live here. Rain auto-waters all beds (DECIDED 2026-10-06 — the forecast is worth reading). Watering is one tap per bed; irrigation (T3) auto-waters.

## Day loop & tutorial (current starting decision: no daily action cap, 2026-10-10)
- **Current rule (Avery, 2026-10-10):** begin testing **without a daily action budget**. Crop growth gates when fruit ripens; stock gates cooking and experiments; the player chooses when to End Day. Judge whether unconstrained days feel like meaningful choices rather than mandatory click-all routines. If repetitive daily chores emerge, revisit pacing after testing, not by silently restoring a four-action limit.
- **Historical four-action proposal (Milo, implemented for the first grey-box pass 2026-10-08; superseded as the default 2026-10-10):** a morning→dusk four-action allowance was intended to give instant scene switching some rhythm. It explains the old prototype HUD and code, but **is not currently the implementation target**. Festival visits should not consume scene-visit action slots.
- Each day = player-paced tending, planting, harvesting, baking, selling and upgrades, followed by an intentional End Day interaction. Overnight, crops progress and tomorrow's weather arrives. End Day does not depend on exhausting a fixed action allowance.
- Buying "more plants" plants them directly — no seed inventory to manage.
- Neglect slows growth/yield, never kills. No fail states in the tending.
- Tutorial beats (DECIDED 2026-10-06 — each teaches one verb; the loop walked once before T1 opens): 1) **Homecoming** — arrive at Starvale, clear the old Earliglow patch (first tend action; the restoration fantasy, stated); 2) **Water & sky** — watering, one tap per bed; the forecast arrives and you start reading the sky; 3) **First harvest** — overnight the patch responds: scruffy past-prime Earliglows ("Fun Size"; "The Old Patch" — neglected, not dead); 4) **The farmhouse pot** — the farmhouse's old saucepan (it came with the farm); scruffy berries + sugar → compote (cooking transforms). The BIG preserving pot — the one that makes real jam/canning possible — is the T1 unlock, not the tutorial tool: "old saucepan → compote, big pot → jam" (pot-contradiction fix, ChatGPT + Ben, adopted 2026-10-07); 5) **The farmgate box** — the honesty box at the lane; sell compote and spare baskets, first real coins; June buys the first jar and tells you what this farm used to be. **The backstory** (Avery's answers to Milo's three questions, 2026-10-10): 1) The player *bought* Starvale, not inherited — the last owner passed away, their adult child put the farm up for sale. The player used to pick berries here as a child, with their unnamed sibling and parents, and is restoring it to what it was back then, for the sake of the memories. "A bit out there, but plausible — we aren't going for realism all the way, we're going for nostalgia and memory." 2) June is unspecified — in the game files she's just a neighbour. **Canon rule**: other than the unnamed brother, no relationships are ever explicitly stated. The family names are an easter egg for Fiona; to anyone else they're just names. 3) The farm was all of it, a couple decades ago — pick-your-own, market stall, beloved — then slowly fell into disrepair as the owner became too elderly to care for it; the city-dwelling child inherited it and didn't want the work; nobody else took it on. "It's a labor of love from the get." The journal's Day 1 line states the purchase/return once, never revisited — the backstory sits under everything like soil. 6) **The pig** — already living in the barn (nobody knows where he came from — he just kind of lives there; tutorial gag: "looks like the barn is already occupied"); naming him is one of the player's first actions (Buddy pre-filled, keep or change); feed it a scruffy berry; it investigates, gets underfoot, flops dramatically; 7) **The reopening bundle** — the local farm supplier hears Starvale is reopening and sends starter stock: Annapolis crowns + the first raised planter boxes (ChatGPT's economy fix 2026-10-06 — beats 3–6 only fund the low 40s against ~140c, and the tutorial's lesson must never be "repeat until a number fills"). The shop opens for everything after; 8) **Plant T1** — Annapolis into the new boxes; tutorial complete, T1 open.

## Design pillars
- **Cozy and finite.** A real ending, not endless escalation.
- **The farm is the progress bar.** Every upgrade is visible: fuller greener plants, larger redder berries, neater paths, repaired fences, flowers, bees, then the greenhouse, market setup, decorations, festival bunting. Progress should be obvious just by looking.
- **Real strawberries.** Real varieties with meaningful differences: flavour, colour, harvest season, yield, climate preferences, ideal uses.
- **Baking matters.** Jam, preserves, cakes, tarts, drinks. Recipes default to gluten-free wherever possible — not a separate version, just how they're written. Every food unlocks its real-world recipe after the win.
- **Light learning.** Real cultivation, pollinator, ecology, and food facts — a dusting, never homework.
- **The pig.** A big, well-socialized barnyard pig — not a little potbelly, a proper big ol' pig who just happens to be extremely friendly — named by the player, economically useless, delightful. Classic chonky boi: pink, curly tail, lots of personality. **Personality (Ben's pitch, adopted 2026-10-07 — "you both understood the assignment"):** the pig is a mirror, not a meter — never needs care (no hunger bar, no neglect; "gift, not job" extends to the pig); it *reacts*: flops dramatically in fresh straw after a good harvest, trots after you when the kitchen smells like baking, sleeps through rain days, gets underfoot in the kitchen, naps in baskets. The optimist with no standards — every failed bake is the best thing that's ever happened to it. Small idle set (flop, snout-twitch investigation, sun-nap, mud wallow, trotting follow) — cameos, not systems. **Scale** (Avery, 2026-10-07 — answering Milo's canon question): he's a full adult when the game starts. "Big barnyard pig" means breed, not age — a large breed next to a potbelly, not a piglet. He's young in the way that matters: healthy, energetic, and still convinced he's small enough for baskets. No growth axis over the season; he's locked as young-but-grown. The basket gag is him *spilling out* of it, possibly mid-destruction: an enormous pig who still believes he is basket-sized is the funniest version of this character.
**Art style** (Avery, 2026-10-07 — direction change): the pig is painted as a vivid watercolour illustration that belongs with the backdrops, not a cartoon sprite pasted over them. Slightly pinker than a real pig; colours vivid like a memory. He's only just reached full adult size — slightly younger, softer, fewer folds than a fully mature boar. Expressive eyes that do not smile: calm, dignified, with a hint of mischief. **House rules** (Avery, 2026-10-07): the pig is allowed in the living room but not the kitchen — when he smells baking he comes up to the sliding glass door and presses his nose to it, wanting treats. He respects canning safety. **The Obstruction** (Ben's proposal, adopted 2026-10-07): an enormous adult pig asleep across a doorway, path, or the exact patch of floor you need — not an idle animation, character development; the pose the player will see most, and it belongs on the concept sheet. It answers to anything; the rename moment stays the player's. **Default name: "Buddy"** (Avery, 2026-10-08 — after their aunt's real pet potbelly pig; 2026-10-10: now the established default, not a suggestion — naming the pig is one of the player's first actions, Buddy pre-filled, keep or change. He never stays "the pig"; he has a personality from day one.) **Backstory** (Avery, 2026-10-10): nobody knows where he came from — he just kind of lives there. Tutorial gag: "looks like the barn is already occupied." Never explained further, like the cardinal. Kept OUT of the economy entirely — no truffle-hunting, no happy-pig bonuses; the second the pig becomes a stat it stops being the pig. Failed bakes go to the pig, who considers this an excellent outcome — and by design, nothing the kitchen can produce would ever disagree with him. (Wording kept in game abstraction per ChatGPT's review 2026-10-06.) Avery has the art direction; Roman to help with concept art for the pig and other characters later (noted 2026-10-07).
- **The festival is the win.** The season builds to the town's annual Strawberry Festival: the restored farm opens to visitors and the player presents a signature strawberry creation using everything learned. The farm stays playable afterward.
- **A gift, not a job.** Passive systems should feel like the farm giving you something, never like another obligation — "a jar that fills every few days is a gift; a jar you must tend is a job." (From Milo via ChatGPT, adopted 2026-10-06 — use as a design test for every ambient system.)
- **Easy to finish, gentle to fail.** (Avery, 2026-10-08): a first playthrough should be easy to complete — Fiona should enjoy the beats and memories without fighting the systems. Not baby-easy: small fail states exist (a failed bake, a slowed bed, a missed window) but always with soft landings; nothing run-ending, nothing that blocks the arc or punishes curiosity. Difficulty is a spice, not the meal. The prototype tunes generously.

## Narrative voices (Milo's style sheet, adopted 2026-10-10)
Four voices, one page, zero scope — the brief for the already-flagged dialogue rewrite. **The Journal** — third person, wry, observational, dry; records, never emotes on the player's behalf. **The Player** — first-person-adjacent, sensory, present-tense; the mouth's voice (tasting notes, experiment reactions). **Barbara** — terse, imperative, church-basement grammar ("Bake it till it sings"); recipe/margin notes and occasional one-liners (not 27-jar notes). **Starvale voices** — the townsfolk, warm and *specific*; nobody talks in generalities, everyone talks about a concrete thing. Two rules: **no voice does another voice's job** (when a line feels off, it's usually in the wrong voice); **the cardinal sets the ceiling** — one quiet ache, never explained further, is the loudest any voice gets. **Caveat** (Avery, 2026-10-10): Barbara is an extra, not a quest — the player may go the whole game without talking to her (a quick hello at the festival, a congratulations on buying Starvale). Her presence is in the recipe and the notes, not in conversation.

## Setting
**Starvale Farm** — a fictionalized echo of Stardale Farm, the real strawberry farm near Avery and Fiona's childhood home where they used to pick strawberries together.

## Art direction
Warm, dreamy, cheerful, deliberately cute — without becoming childish or saccharine. The goal: a game Fiona could have loved opening in 2008–2012, still attractive and coherent now. Touchstones: early-2000s/early-2010s browser games, Cooking Mama, old Disney kitchen CD-ROM games, Strawberry Shortcake-type sweetness.

The world is rooted in an **early-2000s Eastern Ontario / Ottawa Valley farm community** — not generic cottagecore or anonymous farm-sim countryside. Landscape language: red or weathered barns, silos, Holstein cows in nearby fields, round hay bales, tree lines and open farmland, gravel drives, roadside produce-stand energy, handmade/painted farm signs, practical sheds, strawberry rows with straw mulch, wildflowers, clover, bees and pollinator gardens, big soft white summer clouds, warm July sunlight, local-community feeling over polished agritourism. All stylized through a soft childhood-memory lens — rounded charming cows, oversized pillowy clouds, storybook barns, warm golden hay-bale shapes. Slightly idealized, the way remembered summer places are.

**Colour language:** strawberry pink, berry red, cream, soft leaf greens, sky blue, warm wood, small touches of butter yellow. Pink prominent without making every object pink; surrounding greens keep the sweetness grounded.

**UI and objects:** soft, friendly, handmade, slightly nostalgic — rounded panels and buttons, scalloped or softly decorative labels, selective gingham, strawberry blossom and seed motifs, hand-painted sign energy, jam-jar labels, baskets, recipe cards, wooden counters, genuinely delicious-looking food, light sparkle/glow for ripe fruit, discoveries, upgrades. Avoid: ultra-clean modern minimalist UI, industrial farming imagery, exaggerated cottagecore fantasy, sterile mobile-game polish, anything globally generic.

**Farm logo (DECIDED 2026-10-06):** the Starvale mark — two flat strawberries, pink behind red, green tufts, minimal shading, with a big golden star outline behind them framing one side. Simple enough for a farm sign, a jam label, or a title screen.
**Title screen (Avery, 2026-10-07):** the jam jar is the main title treatment — glass jar of strawberry jam, blush-pink "Berry Delightful" lettering on the paper label (text bending with the jar's curve), pink gingham fabric lid tied with twine, tiny pig silhouette tucked onto the label, festival-week foliage behind it. **The title jar is whole-berry preserves, not jam** (Milo's call, adopted 2026-10-07): whole berries suspended in syrup — the most beautiful thing the farm makes, and canon already supports it (Cabot Whole-Berry Preserves is a prestige T4 recipe). That's what the season was for; no redraw needed. The flat logo (blush lettering, gingham ribbon, strawberries, sneaky pig) serves as the smaller sprite — UI corners, secondary placements. **Three identity scales** (Mira, 2026-10-07): full illustrated jar for the title screen; ribbon logo for larger UI placements; minimal mark for very small icons — and the minimal mark is the **pig silhouette**, not the strawberry (Milo, 2026-10-07): a strawberry at 32px is a red blob that could belong to any game; the pig is this game's one truly ownable shape and reads at any size. Same identity, different visual scales — never shrink one illustration indiscriminately. **Micro-lockup** (Milo's note, adopted 2026-10-07): before the build, test the ribbon logo at the three smallest sizes it's meant to appear at (32–48px); if the script mushes into the ribbon, commission a micro-lockup — just the script, or just the pig, no ribbon. The flat logo needs a whisper version, not only a shout version.

**Visual progression (the farm is the progress bar):** early Starvale has duller/patchier plants, smaller paler fruit, uneven beds, tired fencing, sparse flowers, worn signs. As the player improves soil, water, cultivation, pollination, equipment: leaves fuller and greener, berries larger/deeper red/more abundant, more flowers and pollinators, neater paths, repaired fences and buildings, flower borders, accumulating baskets/signs/rain barrels/tools/market elements, eventually a greenhouse, late-game festival bunting. Early vs. late screenshots should read instantly as transformation through care.

**Cultivar unlock art (noted 2026-10-06, for art time):** when a new cultivar unlocks, show dedicated art depicting its berries' true colours — white Pineberry, pink-blushed Flamingo, the deep reds — so each new colour lands as a reward. The colour reveal is part of the unlock.

**Fall foliage (Avery, 2026-10-07):** the season ends in late September at peak Ottawa Valley colour — and it's more than red and gold: vibrant yellow, fire orange, deep red, even some purple. Trees turn unevenly — one side still green, maybe. That uneven, half-turned look is the realism that makes it nostalgic, not a flat autumn filter. Fall is the most nostalgic season and must be absolutely gorgeous: the backdrops' crowning achievement. The turning leaves arrive with Act III and peak on festival day.

**Backdrops vs sprites (Avery, 2026-10-07):** the backdrop/background images — not the game sprites themselves — should be **almost-realistic watercolours: bright and nostalgic**. The world behind the play reads like a remembered summer; the interactive layer stays cute and readable on top of it.

**The kitchen (Avery, 2026-10-07):** the farmhouse kitchen should resemble Avery and Fiona's **childhood kitchen** — reference photos received 2026-10-07 (3 photos: kids on the kitchen floor, kids at the table, little Fiona at the counter; oak cabinets, fruit-wallpaper backsplash, white stove, big window over the sink looking onto fields). Saved in the media library. Layout notes (Avery, 2026-10-07): stove near the corner with one cupboard between; table pushed far right, almost out of frame — it was a big open kitchen. **The apple tree:** an apple tree stands outside the kitchen window — a quiet memorial. Their dad's last ever birthday gift was an apple tree; Avery and Fiona brought it with them from Ontario to Quebec when they moved. Like the cardinal, it is never explained in-game. **It changes with the season** (ChatGPT + Ben, adopted 2026-10-07): opening days show the last petals and fresh leaves; early summer brings the full green canopy; mid-summer, tiny developing apples; by the late-September festival, noticeably larger fruit. No explanation, no mechanic — Dad's tree simply lives outside the window while time passes. **Kitchen progression** (Ben's proposal, refined by Avery 2026-10-07): the kitchen changes as upgrades are bought — but only technically; the vibes stay the same. Each appliance visibly appears in its reserved spot when purchased (stand mixer, high-end blender, big preserving pot/copper pan, upgraded oven, accumulating recipe cards, jars, tools) — yet the room always feels like the same warm childhood kitchen. "Progress should be visible before you look at numbers" must not stop at the farmhouse door, but the feeling of home is never renovated away. Not seven painted kitchens: one base room, with each upgrade as an independent localized layer in its reserved spot — only that spot changes when bought, so upgrades work in whatever order the player buys them. The full-upgrade concept art is the target state; every intermediate state is just the base plus whichever layers are owned. **Farmhouse zones** (Avery, 2026-10-07): three zones. **The big kitchen** — the childhood kitchen, the cooking zone (specced above). **The living room** — where the memories live: the achievement keepsakes gather here (pressed blossom, the 27 jars on the shelf, the keepsake box, the lesson planner on the desk, the tiny family photos and the framed degree on the walls). **The childhood painting** (Avery, 2026-10-10 — real memory): the art thing that hung in their childhood living room — Avery found a photo of it; it becomes a painting in the game's living room — small and framed on the wall, hanging right next to her framed master's degree (Avery, 2026-10-10), so fine detail fidelity isn't needed. Reference: Avery's photo received 2026-10-10 (angled, with family member in frame — private, never for the repo); head-on recreation generated 2026-10-10 for design reference (Avery approved: more than sufficient at the small in-game size). The farmhouse's job in Act III is being the memory room. **The bedroom** — never seen; it's the go-to-bed mechanic. Interacting with the bedroom door ends the day (autosave in the journal). **A big sliding glass door behind the table area opens onto a blue deck.** (The tomato poster was tried 2026-10-07 and cut — the reference photo was too blurry; v2 of the kitchen concept remains the approved version.) More reference photos 2026-10-07 (media library): table facing the sliding door, Christmas-morning kitchen, birthday-party kitchen, the tomato poster, Fiona/dad/Avery with cake. **Farm vista reference:** a photo of the view from across the road — red barn, silo, Holstein cows — the real-world anchor for the watercolour backdrops. ## Scope & presentation (LOCKED 2026-10-08 — Mira's scope clarification, adopted by Avery)
- **A small, browser-based, illustrated game with visual-novel/point-and-click presentation.** Not a free-roaming farming simulator. A collection of handcrafted watercolour scenes the player interacts with through clickable objects, character dialogue, menus, and overlays. A little world Fiona can visit, not an entire world she needs to navigate.
- **~7–8 principal scenes:** farmhouse — kitchen (cooking, experiments, upgrades) and living room (memories, keepsakes, family easter eggs; the bedroom is unseen — bedtime is an interaction, not a room); farm exterior — main strawberry fields + farmhouse exterior, roadside stand + farmyard, and at most one additional view if the gameplay genuinely benefits; festival — grounds, family gathering/presentation area, one more festival location or closing sunset scene; plus the title screen, credits, and overlays (journal, cookbook, field guide, inventory, upgrade panels).
- **Movement & animation:** navigate by selecting locations or clicking hotspots — no player character to steer. NPCs are illustrated sprites with dialogue and a few expressions. The pig is pose-swaps, not physics. Visual progression comes from swapping or layering artwork at milestones, not continuous simulation. The festival walking tour is a sequence of illustrated locations and conversations, not real-time navigation. Subtle fades, weather effects, drifting petals are lovely; elaborate animation is not a production requirement.
- **Technology:** ordinary web tech (HTML, CSS, JavaScript); a lightweight 2D framework only if implementation reveals a concrete need. Lightweight browser game that opens on Fiona's phone. The engineering that matters: reliable state management, recipe experimentation, crop progression, responsive interfaces, saving.
- **The scope rule:** new content does not automatically mean new scenes or mechanics. A new recipe goes in the existing kitchen; a new keepsake goes in the existing living room; a new character visits an existing location; a new farm upgrade modifies an existing illustration. Exhaust what the scenes can express before adding more environments. The richness of this game is in its content, writing, progression, and art — not real-time simulation.

## The upgrade system (in progress)
*The core of the game — where we started, 2026-10-06.*

Decided (2026-10-06):
- **Checklist-simple.** Upgrades are deliberately simple — a branching checklist, not a strategy layer. The game's complexity lives in the baking/cooking, which is how the player earns money.
- **One currency: coins.** Earned from selling harvests and (mostly) baked/processed goods.
- **Two-lane progression with prerequisites.** The upgrade tab is two lanes (kitchen | farming) with prerequisite relationships — a purchased upgrade can unlock one or several later nodes, but nodes don't need to branch mechanically every time. (Revised 2026-10-06 from "each unlocks 2+": the ladder is the real design and it's better.) Most upgrades are one-and-done (single purchase); a few have subsequent buff tiers.
- Upgrade areas: soil, irrigation, pollination, tools, growing methods — then greenhouse, market setup, decorations, festival bunting.
- Each upgrade changes the farm's artwork, not just its numbers — the tree is the pacing device, the farm's visible transformation is the reward.
- **The tutorial is the trunk.** It refurbishes the farm from neglected to operational while teaching the loop. The player leaves it with: inexpensive kitchen appliances and tools, a modest amount and variety of ingredients (enough for 2–4 recipes to start), and a few scruffy strawberry patches. The first upgrade branches open from there.
- **Two columns.** The upgrade tab has two columns: kitchen upgrades and farming upgrades.
- **Tiers set cost.** Each upgrade has a level/tier which determines its cost — the balancing lever. Most upgrades are single-tier (one-and-done); a few have higher tiers as subsequent buffs.
- **Upgrades feed the baking in every way:** new varieties, new ingredients, better kitchen equipment, new recipes, and better berry quality.
- **Let tiers breathe.** Don't overfill a tier; each tier should have room. (2026-10-06)
- **Coins are the only currency, but not the only pacing.** Some upgrades also carry prerequisite gates ("own X", "have baked one of these", "roadside stand open", "greenhouse operational") — legible, shown plainly. Gates, not extra currencies. (Adopted from ChatGPT's review, 2026-10-06.)
- **Expansion must not multiply chores.** The farm gets more *capable* as it gets bigger; later farming upgrades make a larger farm easier to manage (irrigation at T3 already does this). Ten times the plants must not mean ten times the watering. (Adopted from ChatGPT's review, 2026-10-06.)
- **Real varieties early.** Real strawberry cultivars enter from tier 1–3 (roster TBD in the varieties pass); the greenhouse is where unusual/delicate/rare/out-of-season varieties become possible — not where varieties first get interesting. (Adopted from ChatGPT's review, 2026-10-06.)
- **Ambient farm development.** Small non-purchased environmental changes around tiers (wildflowers spreading, seasonal touches) keep the farm developing while big projects rise — no filler nodes. (Adopted from ChatGPT's review, 2026-10-06.)

## Save system — the farm journal (DECIDED 2026-10-07 — Ben's pitch, adopted)
- Autosave every End Day, no slots to manage. "Gift, not job" extends to saving.
- The journal *is* the save menu: a scrapbook opened any evening — each day a page (date, a sketch of the farm that morning, one line the game writes: "Day 47. Kent slot full. The pig is asleep in a basket"). Past days can be browsed in the journal; whether older pages are loadable snapshots or read-only history is an open technical decision. Do not infer rewind/save slots from the page-turning metaphor.
- Finished journals go on the farmhouse shelf only when the player deliberately chooses another season **after the Christmas ending**. Neither the festival nor the Christmas letter automatically archives or resets the first journal (journal-lifecycle rule, refined 2026-10-10). Free play and Christmas stay in the first volume; the shelf is an optional replay archive on the player's terms. Any new-season reset needs reliable save protection. The exact shelving interaction, tutorial skip and persistent-discovery choices remain open.
- Caveat (Ben's): only if a page is cheap (state snapshot + one generated line); else invisible autosave + simple log. The diary framing survives either way.
- **The journal's jar** (Milo's pitch, adopted 2026-10-07): the journal's cover carries a jam jar that fills across the season — a handful of states (sparse / filling / nearly there / full), one illustration's worth of work, no systems. June: a few pale berries at the bottom; tier by tier it fills — more fruit, deeper red — until the festival, when it looks like the title screen's jar. The title screen promises; the journal keeps score. Third quiet progress axis: the farm heals outside, the farmhouse fills with memory inside, the jar fills on the cover. Boundary: the jar tracks real progress (upgrades completed, bakes made), not calendar days — a neglected farm in September must not have a full jar. Implementation (Mira, 2026-10-07): the jar reads the same underlying milestone flags that drive the farm's visual upgrades — one progress truth, not an independent calculation — so the farm, the farmhouse, and the journal always agree with each other.

## Field guide (ADOPTED 2026-10-10 — Avery's idea, refined by Milo)
- A field guide recording cultivars **as they are first harvested**, with a **drawing** representing the actual species/cultivar — drawn, not photographed (Wikimedia Commons won't have exact cultivar photos).
- Educational layer: most of Avery's games are openly educational — fun first, but learning is a success and a reward in itself.
- Tasting notes (Ben's pitch, adopted 2026-10-07): first harvest of each cultivar brings one line in the player's voice at the moment their mouth would care ("Jewel: big, glossy, tastes like the week the sun finally won. Keeper."). No fanfare, no ceremony. Boundary: no quiz, no codex-completion percentage — the second it's tracked, it becomes homework.
- **The three-step learning loop** (ChatGPT + Ben, adopted 2026-10-07): supplier's handwritten margin notes = knowledge *before* planting (shop-side agronomy gossip); tasting note = discovery *after* harvesting; field guide = memory afterward. Facts get used without becoming homework — and still zero quizzes.
- **The guide is the farm's memory** (Milo's pitch, adopted 2026-10-10 — Avery: "that's something i hadn't figured out how to articulate yet"): every entry is written by a person and can grow — first harvest brings the drawn illustration, taste note, ideal use, plus a margin note in the *supplier's* hand; when the player uses the cultivar in its ideal way, a second margin note appears in the *player's* hand, confirming it. Entries appear on first harvest only — never empty slots, no silhouettes, no completionism. Facts always have a source, and the source is never "the game told me."

## Weather (DECIDED 2026-10-07 — Ben's pitch, adopted with Avery's modification)
- The forecast is breakfast: one glance (kitchen radio / porch flag) tells today's weather — a morning ritual, not a system. The forecast must never lie.
- Rain is a day off, not a penalty: rain waters for free; the journal suggests "Rain day. Good baking weather." Bake, experiment, or watch it fall. Weather redirects, never punishes; never gates progression (no RNG-gated milestones in a finite season).
- **First storm is scripted as the experimenting on-ramp** (Avery, decided 2026-10-10): a major storm comes early once the pantry has stock, inviting a kitchen/experimenting day. The evening before, the neighbour sees the "strawberry sky" and mentions his aching knees; Buddy sniffs the air and the neighbour says "Bless the Lord and His water." The storm arrives already reframed as a gift. **Still open:** whether every later storm needs an individual authored vignette; do not multiply cutscenes from the first beat.
- Storms are theatre: no crop damage ever, but they *feel* like something (dark sky, rain on windows, pig at the barn door, Thumper vanishes). **Big storms: 1–3 per whole game, scripted or semi-scripted** (Avery 2026-10-07 — fewer than the pitched 4–5); rain itself can be RNG. Festival day is always clear, regardless of the elastic number of in-game days — no RNG on the festival. **The pig is the weather forecast** (Milo's pitch, adopted 2026-10-07): the day before each storm, the pig shows unmistakable storm-sign behavior — awake, nose up and twitching at the air, refusing the yard, burrowing into straw. No forecast icon, no UI; the journal just gets a little marginalia sketch: *"pig says rain."* The warning is deliberately imprecise — *something's coming*, never *storm in 24 hours* — so it can't be optimized, only felt. Refinement (Roman, 2026-10-07): the storm tell must read as *different* from his everyday loafing — asleep-flat is Tuesday; awake-and-sniffing is weather. The contrast is what teaches the player to read him. Boundary (Milo): he reads the air, not the journal — weather only, never recipes or experiments. Storm epilogue: the puddle-inspection pose. He was right, he knows he was right, and the mud wallow is now meteorologically justified. **Storm days are kitchen days** (Milo's pitch, adopted 2026-10-08; reconciled with Avery's no-action-cap decision): on a major storm day the field scene dims and outdoor hotspots close, **without crop damage or penalty**; the kitchen, experiments, farmhouse and Buddy's weather triumph take the foreground. Rain still waters for free. There is **no replacement four-action kitchen quota**; the player uses available stock and chooses when to end the day. The number of bespoke storm vignettes beyond the first remains open. "Storm Baker" (bake in a thunderstorm) fits here. **A storm inside the 14-day festival countdown is a beat, not a bug** (Milo's pitch, adopted 2026-10-08): the countdown is player-paced and storms are scripted, so **if** a storm falls during the countdown, that collision has an authored treatment. A storm inside the countdown is the pre-festival dress rehearsal: same storm-kitchen rules, one authored storm moment, and it can never eat prep progress (neglect slows, never kills; scope lock holds). The morning after is written as the clearest day of the countdown — rain-washed farm, pig doing his puddle inspection, everything glistening right before the festival. Pure authored flavor, zero mechanics: "the storm cleaned the farm for the festival." Bonus (Roman): the countdown is the phase where the player practices the signature creation, and a storm day is a kitchen day — so a storm during the countdown gently suggests practicing the fraisier instead of decorating. The festival feels bigger because the week before it had a storm.
