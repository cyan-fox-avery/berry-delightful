## T1 — Starter recipes

### 1. Strawberry Compote
*The tutorial bake — the one that teaches you scruffy berries have a destiny.*

**Yield:** about 500 ml (2 cups)

**Ingredients**
- 500 g (4 cups) strawberries, hulled and quartered — the past-prime ones are perfect here
- 60 g (⅓ cup) sugar
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 tbsp water

**Method**
1. Put everything in a saucepan over medium heat. Stir until the sugar dissolves and the berries start releasing their juice.
2. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook 10–15 minutes, stirring now and then, until the berries are soft and the liquid has thickened to a spoon-coating syrup. Mash a few berries against the side of the pan if you like it thicker.
3. Taste and add another spoonful of sugar if your berries were very tart. Let cool — it thickens a little more as it sits.

**Storage:** Refrigerate up to 1 week; freezes well up to 3 months.

*Source/adaptation: standard home-kitchen compote method; written for the tutorial's scruffy Earliglow-style berries.*

### 2. Strawberry Shortcake
*The iconic first real bake — biscuit, macerated berries, cream.*

**Yield:** 6 shortcakes

**Ingredients — biscuits**
- 250 g (2½ cups) fine blanched almond flour
- 2 tbsp coconut flour
- ½ tsp baking soda
- ¼ tsp fine salt
- 2 large eggs, room temperature
- 45 g (3 tbsp) butter, melted (or coconut oil)
- 60 g (3 tbsp) honey or maple syrup
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**Ingredients — berries & cream**
- 500 g (4 cups) strawberries, sliced
- 1–2 tbsp sugar
- 250 ml (1 cup) whipping cream
- 1 tbsp sugar, for the cream

**Method**
1. Toss the sliced strawberries with the 1–2 tbsp sugar and set aside to macerate at least 20 minutes (an hour is even better) — they'll make their own syrup.
2. Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Line a baking sheet with parchment.
3. Whisk the almond flour, coconut flour, baking soda, and salt in a large bowl.
4. In a smaller bowl, whisk the eggs, melted butter, honey, and vanilla. Stir the wet into the dry until a soft dough forms.
5. Scoop 6 mounds (about ¼ cup each) onto the sheet and flatten slightly. Bake 13–16 minutes, until golden on top and set in the middle. Cool a few minutes.
6. Whip the cream with the 1 tbsp sugar to soft peaks.
7. Split each warm biscuit, spoon over berries and their syrup, add a cloud of cream, and cap with the top half.

**Storage:** Biscuits keep 2 days in an airtight container (refresh 5 minutes in a warm oven). Assemble just before serving.

**GF note:** The biscuit is almond-flour based — no wheat flour at all, so this simply *is* the recipe, not an adaptation of one.

*Source/adaptation: biscuit method adapted from GF almond-flour shortcake recipes (Life Made Sweeter; GlutenFreeFix); assembly is the classic.*

### 3. Fresh Berry Smoothie
*The blender's first job — aromatic berries, nothing to hide behind.*

**Yield:** about 750 ml (2 large glasses)

**Ingredients**
- 300 g (2 cups) fresh strawberries, hulled
- 250 ml (1 cup) plain yogurt
- 125 ml (½ cup) milk (any kind)
- 1–2 tbsp honey, to taste
- A handful of ice (optional, for extra chill)

**Method**
1. Put everything in the blender.
2. Blend until completely smooth, about 30–45 seconds. Taste and add the second spoonful of honey if your berries are tart.
3. Pour and drink immediately.

**Storage:** Best fresh. Keeps a few hours in the fridge (shake before drinking).

*Source/adaptation: classic smoothie ratios; written as the forerunner of the T3 frozen-Kent smoothie.*

### 4. Basic Strawberry Jam
*The classic. It cannot wait until tier 3 and neither should you.*

**Yield:** 8 × 250 ml jars (about 2 L)

**Ingredients**
- 1.25 kg (5 cups / 1250 ml) crushed strawberries (from about 2.5 quarts whole berries)
- 1.4 kg (7 cups) granulated sugar
- 60 ml (4 tbsp) lemon juice
- 1 pkg (57 g) Bernardin Original Fruit Pectin
- ½ tsp (2 ml) butter or margarine (optional — reduces foaming)

**Method**
1. Set up the canner: place 8 clean 250 ml mason jars on the rack in a boiling-water canner, cover with water, and heat to a simmer (82°C / 180°F). Keep the jars hot until filling. Wash the lids in hot, soapy water, dry, and set aside at room temperature — do not preheat lids. Set the screw bands aside.
2. Wash, hull, and crush the strawberries one layer at a time. Measure out 5 cups (1250 ml) crushed fruit.
3. Measure the sugar into a bowl; set aside.
4. In a large, deep stainless-steel saucepan, combine the crushed strawberries, lemon juice, and butter. Whisk in the pectin until dissolved.
5. Over high heat, bring the mixture to a full rolling boil. Add all of the sugar at once. Stirring constantly, return to a full rolling boil that cannot be stirred down. Boil hard for 1 minute, stirring constantly. Remove from heat and skim off any foam.
6. Quickly ladle the hot jam into a hot jar, leaving ¼ inch (0.5 cm) headspace. Slide a nonmetallic utensil around the inside to release air bubbles and adjust headspace if needed. Wipe the jar rim clean, centre a lid on it, and screw the band down until resistance is met, then just to fingertip-tight. Return the jar to the canner. Repeat with the remaining jam.
7. Make sure all jars are covered by at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) of water. Cover the canner, bring to a full rolling boil, then start timing: process 10 minutes at altitudes up to 305 m (1,000 ft). Above that, add 5 minutes for 305–915 m (1,001–3,000 ft) and 10 minutes above 915 m.
8. When the time is up, turn off the heat, remove the canner lid, wait 5 minutes, then lift the jars straight out without tilting. Set upright on a protected surface and cool, undisturbed, for 24 hours — do not retighten the bands.
9. Check the seals: lids should curve downward and not flex when pressed. Remove the bands, wipe the jars, label, and store.

**Storage:** Sealed jars keep in a cool, dark place; best quality within 1 year. Refrigerate after opening and use within 1 month. Any jar that did not seal goes straight to the fridge — use within 3 weeks.

*Source: formulation and processing follow Bernardin’s tested “Classic ‘Easy’ Strawberry Jam” (bernardin.ca). For shelf-stable canning, do not change ingredient quantities — the tested formulation is what makes it safe.*

## T2 — High-end mixer era

### 5. Jam Thumbprints
*Recipes that eat other recipes — your T1 jam becomes a T2 ingredient.*

**Yield:** about 22 small cookies

**Ingredients**
- 190 g (2¼ cups, spooned and levelled) fine blanched almond flour
- 80 g (6 tbsp) butter, melted but not too hot
- 60 ml (¼ cup) maple syrup
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- ¼ tsp fine salt
- 2 tbsp sugar, for rolling
- 80 ml (⅓ cup) strawberry jam (recipe #4!), well stirred

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Line a baking sheet with parchment.
2. Stir the almond flour, melted butter, maple syrup, vanilla, and salt in a medium bowl until uniformly blended and smooth.
3. Put the 2 tbsp sugar in a small bowl. Scoop 2-teaspoon portions of dough, roll into balls between your palms, roll each in the sugar, and set on the sheet about 5 cm (2 inches) apart.
4. Press a thumbprint (or the back of a teaspoon) into the centre of each ball. Smooth any cracks around the edges with your finger — they get more pronounced in the oven.
5. Fill each indent with a level ½ teaspoon of jam. No more — it spreads.
6. Bake 13–15 minutes, until lightly golden at the edges. Cool on the sheet 10 minutes (they're fragile while hot), then move to a rack.

**Storage:** Airtight container at room temperature, 4–5 days.

**GF note:** Almond flour does the whole job here — these were never anything but gluten-free.

*Source/adaptation: adapted from Cookie and Kate's almond-flour thumbprint cookies (cookieandkate.com); the jam-centre idea is the game's own production chain.*

### 6. Honey Strawberry Cake
*The cross-column showcase — the beehive's gift becomes the cake's soul.*

**Yield:** one 20 cm (8-inch) cake, 8–10 slices

**Ingredients**
- 115 g (½ cup) butter, softened
- 80 g (⅓ cup + 1 tbsp) sugar
- 3 large eggs, room temperature
- 120 ml (½ cup) runny honey, plus more for drizzling
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 250 g (2½ cups) fine blanched almond flour
- 1 tsp gluten-free baking powder
- ¼ tsp fine salt
- 200 g (about 1½ cups) strawberries, quartered, tossed with 1 tbsp almond flour

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Grease a 20 cm (8-inch) round pan and line the base with parchment.
2. Cream the butter and sugar with a mixer 3–4 minutes until pale and fluffy — this is the creaming method the high-end mixer was bought for.
3. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the honey and vanilla.
4. Fold in the almond flour, baking powder, and salt until just combined. Fold in the floured strawberries.
5. Spread into the pan and bake 30–35 minutes, until golden and a skewer comes out with just a few moist crumbs.
6. Cool 10 minutes in the pan, then turn out. Serve warm or at room temperature with a drizzle of honey.

**Storage:** Room temperature 2 days (covered), refrigerator up to 5 days. Lovely very slightly warm.

**GF note:** Almond flour replaces wheat flour entirely; the eggs and the creaming give it lift and structure.

*Source/adaptation: almond-honey cake structure informed by GF almond honey cakes (fresh.co.nz; lettyskitchen.com); creamed butter-sugar base per the game's T2 mixer beat.*

### 7. Strawberry Custard Tart
*The pretty one — the first bake that's really about showing off a berry.*

**Yield:** one 23 cm (9-inch) tart, 8 slices

**Ingredients — press-in crust**
- 200 g (2 cups) fine blanched almond flour
- 50 g (¼ cup) sugar
- ¼ tsp fine salt
- 80 g (⅓ cup) butter, melted
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**Ingredients — vanilla custard**
- 500 ml (2 cups) milk
- 4 large egg yolks
- 60 g (¼ cup + 1 tbsp) sugar
- 2 tbsp cornstarch
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

**Ingredients — topping**
- 400 g (about 3 cups) strawberries, hulled and sliced
- 2 tbsp strawberry jam (recipe #4 again!), warmed with 1 tsp water

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F).
2. **Crust:** Stir the almond flour, sugar, salt, melted butter, and vanilla until it clumps like wet sand. Press firmly and evenly into a 23 cm (9-inch) tart pan with a removable bottom, up the sides. Line with parchment, fill with pie weights or dried beans, and blind-bake 12 minutes. Remove weights and parchment; bake 5–8 minutes more until golden. Cool completely.
3. **Custard:** Whisk the yolks, sugar, cornstarch, and salt in a saucepan until smooth. Warm the milk until steaming, then whisk it in gradually. Cook over medium heat, whisking constantly, until thick and just bubbling, 1–2 minutes. Off the heat, stir in the vanilla. Press plastic wrap directly on the surface and chill until cool.
4. Spread the cool custard into the cool shell. Arrange the sliced strawberries over the top in overlapping rows.
5. Brush the berries with the warmed jam glaze for shine. Chill at least 30 minutes before slicing.

**Storage:** Refrigerate; best within 2 days (the shell softens after that).

**GF note:** Press-in almond crust instead of rolled wheat pastry — no rolling pin, no cracking, no fuss.

*Source/adaptation: press-in almond crust in the spirit of GF tart crusts (bojongourmet.com; easyglutenfreedesserts.com); the custard is standard vanilla pastry cream; the jam glaze is the game's second recipe-chain beat.*

### 12. Basic Strawberry Pie
*The original — the honest pie every deluxe version is measured against.*

**Yield:** one 23 cm (9-inch) pie, 8 slices

**Ingredients — crust**
- 190 g (1½ cups) gluten-free all-purpose blend (one with xanthan gum, e.g. Bob's Red Mill 1-to-1 or King Arthur Measure for Measure)
- ¼ tsp fine salt
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 115 g (½ cup) cold butter, cubed
- 60–80 ml (4–5 tbsp) ice water

**Ingredients — filling**
- 750 g (about 6 cups) strawberries, hulled and halved (quartered if large)
- 100 g (½ cup) sugar, plus more to taste
- 3 tbsp cornstarch
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

**Ingredients — crumb top**
- 100 g (¾ cup) gluten-free all-purpose blend
- 60 g (⅓ cup) brown sugar
- 50 g (½ cup) certified gluten-free rolled oats
- ¼ tsp cinnamon (optional)
- 80 g (⅓ cup) butter, melted

**Method**
1. **Crust:** Whisk the blend, salt, and sugar. Cut in the cold butter with a pastry cutter or your fingers until pea-sized crumbs. Add ice water a spoonful at a time until the dough just holds together. Flatten into a disc, wrap, and chill 30 minutes.
2. Heat the oven to 190°C (375°F). Roll the dough between two sheets of parchment to a 30 cm (12-inch) round — GF dough is fragile; the parchment does the lifting. Peel off the top sheet, flip into the pie plate, and peel off the other. Ease in, trim, and crimp the edge. Chill while you make the filling.
3. **Filling:** Toss the berries with the sugar, cornstarch, lemon juice, vanilla, and salt. Let sit 15 minutes so the juices start flowing.
4. **Crumb:** Stir the blend, brown sugar, oats, and cinnamon; pour in the melted butter and toss until clumps form.
5. Fill the shell with the berries and their juice. Scatter the crumb evenly over the top.
6. Bake 45–55 minutes, until the crumb is deep golden and the filling bubbles thickly through the cracks. If the edge browns too fast, shield it with foil. Cool at least 3 hours so the filling sets — the hardest step.

**Storage:** Room temperature 1 day, refrigerated up to 4 days. Reheats beautifully.

**GF note:** A 1-to-1 GF blend does the whole job — crust and crumb — and cornstarch (not flour) thickens the filling. The oats must be *certified* gluten-free; regular oats are cross-contaminated.

*Source/adaptation: composed from classic crumb-top strawberry pie method; GF blend + cornstarch + certified GF oats per the cookbook's flour-diversification rule.*

### 13. Empire Cookies
*Every December, the tin came out. Jam in the middle, icing on top, a cherry if you were lucky. These are the ones she made — this one's for her.*

**Yield:** about 18 sandwich cookies

**Ingredients — cookies**
- 280 g (2¼ cups) gluten-free all-purpose blend (with xanthan gum)
- ¼ tsp fine salt
- 225 g (1 cup) butter, softened
- 75 g (⅔ cup) icing sugar, sifted
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**Ingredients — filling & icing**
- 120 ml (½ cup) strawberry jam (recipe #4 — the chain continues)
- 150 g (1¼ cups) icing sugar, sifted
- 1–2 tbsp milk
- ¼ tsp almond extract (optional)
- 9 glacé cherries, halved (optional but traditional)

**Method**
1. Whisk the blend and salt. Cream the butter and icing sugar 2–3 minutes until fluffy; beat in the vanilla. Stir in the dry ingredients until a soft dough forms. Divide in two, flatten into discs, wrap, and chill 30 minutes (GF dough handles better cold).
2. Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Line two baking sheets with parchment.
3. Roll one disc between parchment to about 5 mm (¼ inch) thick. Cut rounds with a 5 cm (2-inch) cutter, re-rolling scraps once. Place 2 cm apart on the sheets.
4. Bake 10–12 minutes, until just set and barely golden at the edges — they should stay pale. Cool completely on the sheets (they firm up as they cool).
5. Sandwich pairs with about 1 tsp jam each. Whisk the icing sugar, 1 tbsp milk, and almond extract into a thick, spreadable icing; add milk a few drops at a time. Ice the top of each sandwich and crown with half a cherry.

**Storage:** Airtight container at room temperature, 4–5 days. The jam softens them slightly by day two — nobody minds.

**GF note:** A rolled GF-blend shortbread — the blend (not almond flour) gives the classic empire-biscuit snap. This is the memorial recipe; it had to taste like the tin.

*Source/adaptation: classic Canadian empire-biscuit method (the Canadian Living-style holiday cookie); GF blend substituted for wheat flour, strawberry jam in place of the traditional raspberry — the game's berries, her cookie.*

## T3 — Drinks & serious preserves

### 8. Sparkling Strawberry Lemonade
*The flavour favourite's drink — bright, simple, roadside-stand energy.*

**Yield:** about 1.25 L (4–5 glasses)

**Ingredients**
- 400 g (3 cups) strawberries, hulled
- 125 ml (½ cup) lemon juice (2–3 lemons)
- 100 g (½ cup) sugar, plus more to taste
- 250 ml (1 cup) still water
- 750 ml (3 cups) sparkling water, chilled
- Ice and extra strawberry slices, to serve

**Method**
1. Blend the strawberries, lemon juice, sugar, and still water until completely smooth.
2. Strain through a fine sieve into a jug, pressing to get all the juice. Taste — add another spoonful of sugar if you like it sweeter. Chill until cold.
3. Fill glasses with ice, pour in the strawberry base about two-thirds full, and top with sparkling water. Stir once, gently, and garnish with a strawberry slice.

**Storage:** The unsparkling base keeps 3 days refrigerated — add the sparkle only when serving.

*Source/adaptation: standard blended-lemonade method; the high-end blender does the work the game promises.*

### 9. Frozen Kent Smoothie
*The three-tier payoff — plant, freeze, blend. Thick enough to stand a spoon in.*

**Yield:** 2 large glasses

**Ingredients**
- 300 g (2 cups) strawberries, frozen solid (freeze in a single layer on a tray first, then bag)
- 250 ml (1 cup) Greek yogurt
- 125 ml (½ cup) milk (any kind) — start with less; frozen berries need less liquid
- 2 tbsp honey, plus more to taste

**Method**
1. Put the yogurt, milk, and honey in the blender first (liquid at the bottom helps everything move), then add the frozen berries.
2. Blend, tamping down as needed, until completely smooth and thick — 45–60 seconds. Add a splash more milk only if the blender is struggling.
3. Taste for sweetness, pour, and drink immediately. This one waits for no one.

**Storage:** Serve right away — it melts into a thinner (still delicious) drink within 20 minutes.

*Source/adaptation: standard thick-smoothie ratios; the "freeze in a single layer" tip is what keeps the berries from becoming one solid brick.*

### 10. Jewel Jam
*Varietal jam, first of the line — the flavour favourite gets its identity.*

**Yield:** 4 × 250 ml jars (about 1 L)

**Ingredients**
- 1 kg (4 cups / 1000 ml) crushed strawberries — the ripest, most fragrant you can find
- 250 ml (1 cup) unsweetened apple or white grape juice
- 1 pkg (49 g) Bernardin No Sugar Needed Fruit Pectin
- 300 g (1½ cups) granulated sugar

**Method**
1. Set up the canner: place 4 clean 250 ml mason jars on the rack in a boiling-water canner, cover with water, and heat to a simmer (82°C / 180°F). Keep the jars hot until filling. Wash the lids in hot, soapy water, dry, and set aside at room temperature — do not preheat lids. Set the screw bands aside.
2. Taste a berry first — this jam is about the fruit, so start with fruit worth showcasing. Wash, hull, and crush the strawberries one layer at a time; measure 4 cups (1000 ml) into a large, deep stainless-steel saucepan with the juice. Whisk in the pectin until dissolved.
3. Measure the sugar; set aside. (Yes, only 1½ cups — a great berry needs less help, and this lower sugar level is safe *only* because this is the tested No Sugar Needed pectin formulation. Never reduce sugar in a regular-pectin jam recipe.)
4. Over high heat, bring the fruit mixture to a boil, stirring constantly. Add the sugar and return to a boil. Boil 3 minutes, stirring frequently. Remove from heat; skim off foam.
5. Quickly ladle the hot jam into a hot jar, leaving ¼ inch (0.5 cm) headspace. Slide a nonmetallic utensil around the inside to release air bubbles and adjust headspace if needed. Wipe the jar rim clean, centre a lid on it, and screw the band down until resistance is met, then just to fingertip-tight. Return the jar to the canner. Repeat with the remaining jam.
6. Make sure all jars are covered by at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) of water. Cover the canner, bring to a full rolling boil, then start timing: process 10 minutes at altitudes up to 305 m (1,000 ft). Above that, add 5 minutes for 305–915 m (1,001–3,000 ft) and 10 minutes above 915 m.
7. When the time is up, turn off the heat, remove the canner lid, wait 5 minutes, then lift the jars straight out without tilting. Set upright on a protected surface and cool, undisturbed, for 24 hours — do not retighten the bands.
8. Check the seals: lids should curve downward and not flex when pressed. Remove the bands, wipe the jars, label, and store.

**Storage:** Sealed jars keep in a cool, dark place; best quality within 1 year. Refrigerate after opening and use within 1 month. Unsealed jars go to the fridge — use within 3 weeks.

*Source: formulation and processing follow Bernardin’s tested “Light Strawberry Jam – No Sugar Needed Pectin” (bernardin.ca). The reduced sugar is safe only with this tested pectin — do not improvise sugar levels in canning recipes.*

### 14. Strawberry Sorbet
*The freezer hides a dessert — cold, sweet, nothing creamy.*

**Yield:** about 750 ml (3 cups)

**Ingredients**
- 600 g (4 cups) strawberries, frozen solid (freeze hulled berries in a single layer on a tray first, then bag)
- 80–100 g (¼–⅓ cup) honey, to taste
- 2 tbsp lemon juice
- 2–3 tbsp cold water, as needed

**Method**
1. Let the frozen berries sit 5 minutes — just enough that a knife tip goes in, not enough to thaw.
2. Blitz in a food processor with the honey, lemon juice, and 2 tbsp water, scraping down once or twice, until completely smooth and thick. Add the last spoonful of water only if the blade is struggling.
3. Taste: tart berries want the full ⅓ cup of honey; sweet ones need less. Serve soft right away, or freeze 2 hours for scoopable firmness (let it sit 10 minutes before scooping).

**Storage:** Freezer up to 1 month in an airtight container; press parchment on the surface to prevent ice crystals.

**GF note:** Naturally gluten-free — fruit, honey, lemon. Nothing to substitute.

*Source/adaptation: composed from standard no-churn sorbet method; honey as the sole sweetener per the game's frozen-berry + honey combo.*

## T4 — The copper pan (advanced preserves)

### 15. Cabot Whole-Berry Preserves
*The show-off jar — whole berries, glistening, suspended in syrup. The copper pan's reason for existing.*

**Yield:** 4 × 250 ml jars (about 1 L)

**Ingredients**
- 1.5 L (6 cups) small whole strawberries, hulled (choose the smallest, firmest berries — they hold their shape)
- 800 g (4 cups) granulated sugar
- 30 ml (2 tbsp) lemon juice

**Method**
1. Set up the canner: place 4 clean 250 ml mason jars on the rack in a boiling-water canner, cover with water, and heat to a simmer (82°C / 180°F). Keep the jars hot until filling. Wash the lids in hot, soapy water, dry, and set aside at room temperature — do not preheat lids. Set the screw bands aside.
2. Layer the berries and sugar in a large, deep stainless-steel saucepan or your copper preserving pan. Let stand 2–3 hours (or overnight in the fridge) — the sugar draws out the juice and the berries firm up, which is what keeps them whole.
3. Add the lemon juice. Over medium-high heat, bring slowly to a boil, stirring gently so as not to crush the berries. Boil gently, uncovered, 30–40 minutes, skimming foam as it rises, until the syrup thickens and reaches gel stage: it should sheet off a spoon, or wrinkle when a spoonful is chilled on a freezer plate for a minute.
4. Quickly ladle the hot preserves into a hot jar, leaving ¼ inch (0.5 cm) headspace. Slide a nonmetallic utensil around the inside to release air bubbles and adjust headspace if needed. Wipe the jar rim clean, centre a lid on it, and screw the band down until resistance is met, then just to fingertip-tight. Return the jar to the canner. Repeat with the remaining preserves.
5. Make sure all jars are covered by at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) of water. Cover the canner, bring to a full rolling boil, then start timing: process 10 minutes at altitudes up to 305 m (1,000 ft). Above that, add 5 minutes for 305–915 m (1,001–3,000 ft) and 10 minutes above 915 m.
6. When the time is up, turn off the heat, remove the canner lid, wait 5 minutes, then lift the jars straight out without tilting. Set upright on a protected surface and cool, undisturbed, for 24 hours — do not retighten the bands.
7. Check the seals: lids should curve downward and not flex when pressed. Remove the bands, wipe the jars, label, and store.

**Storage:** Sealed jars keep in a cool, dark place; best quality within 1 year. Refrigerate after opening and use within 1 month. Any jar that did not seal goes straight to the fridge — use within 3 weeks.

*Source: long-boil no-pectin method per Bernardin's tested preserve technique (cf. their Strawberry Margarita Preserve, bernardin.ca — fruit + sugar + lemon boiled to gel stage). No improvised sugar quantities — the tested ratio is what makes it safe.*

### 16. Yamaska Vanilla Preserve
*The luxury preserve — quietly French, which will matter later.*

**Yield:** 8 × 250 ml jars (about 2 L)

**Ingredients**
- 1.25 kg (5 cups / 1250 ml) strawberries, hulled and halved (from about 2.5 quarts whole berries)
- 1.4 kg (7 cups) granulated sugar
- 60 ml (4 tbsp) lemon juice
- 1 whole vanilla bean, split lengthwise (or 1 tbsp pure vanilla extract, added off the heat)
- 1 pkg (57 g) Bernardin Original Fruit Pectin
- ½ tsp (2 ml) butter or margarine (optional — reduces foaming)

**Method**
1. Set up the canner: place 8 clean 250 ml mason jars on the rack in a boiling-water canner, cover with water, and heat to a simmer (82°C / 180°F). Keep the jars hot until filling. Wash the lids in hot, soapy water, dry, and set aside at room temperature — do not preheat lids. Set the screw bands aside.
2. Measure the sugar into a bowl; set aside.
3. In a large, deep stainless-steel saucepan, combine the halved strawberries, lemon juice, and butter. Scrape the vanilla seeds into the pot and drop in the split pod. Whisk in the pectin until dissolved.
4. Over high heat, bring the mixture to a full rolling boil. Add all of the sugar at once. Stirring constantly, return to a full rolling boil that cannot be stirred down. Boil hard for 1 minute, stirring constantly. Remove from heat, fish out the vanilla pod, and skim off any foam. (If using extract instead of a bean, stir it in now, off the heat.)
5. Quickly ladle the hot preserve into a hot jar, leaving ¼ inch (0.5 cm) headspace. Slide a nonmetallic utensil around the inside to release air bubbles and adjust headspace if needed. Wipe the jar rim clean, centre a lid on it, and screw the band down until resistance is met, then just to fingertip-tight. Return the jar to the canner. Repeat with the remaining preserve.
6. Make sure all jars are covered by at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) of water. Cover the canner, bring to a full rolling boil, then start timing: process 10 minutes at altitudes up to 305 m (1,000 ft). Above that, add 5 minutes for 305–915 m (1,001–3,000 ft) and 10 minutes above 915 m.
7. When the time is up, turn off the heat, remove the canner lid, wait 5 minutes, then lift the jars straight out without tilting. Set upright on a protected surface and cool, undisturbed, for 24 hours — do not retighten the bands.
8. Check the seals: lids should curve downward and not flex when pressed. Remove the bands, wipe the jars, label, and store.

**Storage:** Sealed jars keep in a cool, dark place; best quality within 1 year. Refrigerate after opening and use within 1 month. Any jar that did not seal goes straight to the fridge — use within 3 weeks.

*Source: Bernardin's tested "Classic 'Easy' Strawberry Jam" formulation (bernardin.ca) with halved berries and a vanilla bean. The vanilla is a flavouring addition only — sugar, acid, and processing are unchanged from the tested recipe, which is what keeps it safe.*

### 19. Strawberry Layer Cake
*"Bigger, not industrial" made edible — the bigger oven's showpiece.*

**Yield:** one 20 cm (8-inch) two-layer cake, 10–12 slices

**Ingredients — cake**
- 280 g (2¼ cups) gluten-free all-purpose blend (with xanthan gum)
- 2 tsp gluten-free baking powder
- ½ tsp baking soda
- ½ tsp fine salt
- 170 g (¾ cup) butter, softened
- 200 g (1 cup) sugar
- 3 large eggs, room temperature
- 2 tsp vanilla extract
- 180 ml (¾ cup) milk (any kind), room temperature

**Ingredients — filling & frosting**
- 400 g (3 cups) strawberries, hulled and sliced
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 375 ml (1½ cups) whipping cream
- 2 tbsp icing sugar, sifted
- 1 tsp vanilla extract

**Method**
1. Heat the oven to 175°C (350°F). Grease two 20 cm (8-inch) round pans and line the bases with parchment.
2. Whisk the blend, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
3. Cream the butter and sugar 3–4 minutes until pale and fluffy. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then the vanilla.
4. Add the dry ingredients in three goes, alternating with the milk, beginning and ending with dry. Mix until just combined — GF batter toughens if overbeaten.
5. Divide between the pans and bake 25–30 minutes, until a skewer comes out clean. Cool 10 minutes in the pans, then turn out and cool completely.
6. Toss the sliced berries with the 2 tbsp sugar; rest 20 minutes until syrupy. Whip the cream with the icing sugar and vanilla to firm peaks.
7. Set one layer on a plate. Spread with a third of the cream, spoon over half the berries and their syrup. Top with the second layer, then the remaining cream and berries. Serve within a few hours for the prettiest slice.

**Storage:** Refrigerate up to 2 days; bring to room temperature 20 minutes before serving.

**GF note:** 1-to-1 GF blend + the creaming method — the structure comes from eggs and air, not gluten. Don't overmix, don't overbake; GF cake dries out faster than wheat.

*Source/adaptation: composed from classic GF vanilla layer-cake method; the berries-and-cream filling is the summer classic.*

### 22. Strawberry Charlotte
*The dessert that shares her name — ladyfingers, strawberry mousse, and a ribbon. The breeding progression you can eat.*

**Yield:** one 20 cm (8-inch) charlotte, 8–10 slices

**Ingredients**
- About 24 gluten-free ladyfingers (savoiardi) — certified GF, e.g. Schär (check the label; you'll trim them to height)
- 500 g (4 cups) strawberries, hulled, plus 200 g extra for decorating
- 100 g (½ cup) sugar, plus 2 tbsp for the berries
- 2½ tsp (1 packet / 7 g) unflavoured gelatin
- 60 ml (¼ cup) cold water
- 250 ml (1 cup) whipping cream
- Ribbon, for tying (the classic finish)

**Method**
1. Line a 20 cm (8-inch) springform pan: stand ladyfingers upright around the inside edge (trim the bottoms so they stand level), sugared side out, packed snugly. Line the base with ladyfingers too, trimming to fit. Set aside.
2. Toss the 200 g decorating berries with the 2 tbsp sugar; set aside.
3. Purée the 500 g berries with the 100 g sugar until smooth; strain out the seeds if you like it silky. You should have about 500 ml (2 cups) purée.
4. Sprinkle the gelatin over the cold water; rest 5 minutes. Warm 125 ml (½ cup) of the purée until steaming, stir in the bloomed gelatin until dissolved, then stir back into the remaining purée. Chill, stirring now and then, until it just begins to thicken — like loose jam, 20–30 minutes. (If it sets firm, you've gone too far; warm it gently back to liquid.)
5. Whip the cream to soft peaks and fold into the thickening purée in three additions.
6. Pour the mousse into the ladyfinger shell. Chill at least 6 hours or overnight, until firmly set.
7. Unmould: run a thin knife around the edge, release the springform. Pile the sugared berries on top and tie the ribbon around the ladyfingers. Slice with a hot, dry knife.

**Storage:** Refrigerate; best within 2 days.

**GF note:** Certified GF ladyfingers do the architecture — everything else (mousse, berries, cream) is naturally gluten-free.

*Source/adaptation: classic charlotte aux fraises method; the only substitution is certified GF ladyfingers for wheat ones.*

### 24. Pineberry Pavlova
*White meringue, white berries, red seeds — the white showpiece. Stunning, and it was never anything but gluten-free.*

**Ingredients — meringue**

### 26. Gariguette Fraisier

**Yield:** one 20 cm (8-inch) fraisier, 10–12 slices

**Ingredients — GF sponge**

- 120 g (⅔ cup) sugar
- 120 g (1 cup) gluten-free all-purpose blend (with xanthan gum)
- Pinch of salt
- 30 g (2 tbsp) butter, melted and cooled

**Ingredients — mousseline cream**
- 500 ml (2 cups) milk
- 4 large egg yolks
- 100 g (½ cup) sugar
- 40 g (¼ cup) cornstarch
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 150 g (⅔ cup) butter, softened

**Ingredients — assembly**

**Method**

### 28. The Barbara Special
*The one Barbara makes when the whole family comes home — latticework, egg wash, sugar sparkling on top. This is what the basic pie grows up into.*

**Yield:** one 23 cm (9-inch) lattice pie, 8 slices

**Ingredients — double crust**
- 380 g (3 cups) gluten-free all-purpose blend (with xanthan gum)
- 1 tbsp sugar
- 1 tsp fine salt
- 225 g (1 cup) cold butter, cubed
- 120–150 ml (½–⅔ cup) ice water

**Ingredients — filling**
- 900 g (about 7 cups) strawberries, hulled and halved
- 120 g (⅔ cup) sugar, plus more to taste
- 4 tbsp cornstarch
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

**Ingredients — finish**
- 1 egg beaten with 1 tbsp milk (egg wash)
- Coarse sugar, for sprinkling

**Method**
1. **Crust:** Whisk the blend, sugar, and salt. Cut in the cold butter to pea-sized crumbs. Add ice water a spoonful at a time until the dough holds together. Divide into two discs (one slightly larger for the base), wrap, and chill at least 1 hour — cold dough is everything with GF pastry.
2. Heat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Roll the larger disc between parchment to a 30 cm (12-inch) round and line a 23 cm (9-inch) pie plate. Chill again while you roll the second disc and cut it into 2.5 cm (1-inch) strips for the lattice. (If a strip cracks, pinch it back together — the lattice forgives.)
3. **Filling:** Toss the berries with the sugar, cornstarch, lemon juice, vanilla, and salt; rest 15 minutes.
4. Fill the shell with the berries and their juice. Weave the strips over the top in a lattice, trim, and crimp the edges. Brush generously with egg wash and sprinkle with coarse sugar.
5. Bake 20 minutes at 200°C (400°F), then reduce to 180°C (350°F) and bake 30–35 minutes more, until the lattice is deep golden and the filling bubbles thickly through the gaps. Shield the edges with foil if they brown too fast. Cool at least 4 hours — a lattice pie served too soon is a beautiful soup.

**Storage:** Room temperature 1 day, refrigerated up to 4 days.

**GF note:** Double GF-blend crust, handled cold and rolled between parchment — the two tricks that make GF lattice possible. Cornstarch thickens; the egg wash and coarse sugar do the glamour.

*Source/adaptation: composed from classic lattice strawberry pie method; this is the deluxe counterpart to recipe #12 — same soul, dressed for the festival.*
